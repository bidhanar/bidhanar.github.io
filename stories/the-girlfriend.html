The Girlfriend

Download PDF Back to Stories

He sat at his desk, head laid down, apparently taking a break from the work he had been doing all morning. Did the work excite him? He didn’t know. John hadn’t really focused on anything since she left. Usually, it didn’t bother him a lot, but on a few days, it became unbearable. It had been a few months since her departure, but how does a human mind know when to stop thinking about something that was, about something that probably never will be again? It never actually does. The only way is to somehow overwrite this figment of someone with something else. John had tried. He was exhausted now.

He got up from his chair and moved to the window, looking at the world, apparently happy, probably because they hadn’t lost their love. He could call her back; it was just a decision away. But no. He had promised her he wouldn’t. He had promised himself he wouldn’t. He needed to distract himself from her, and so he just took his things and left work midday. His manager, having known him for a few months, understood his dilemma and didn’t stop him.

He went straight to the bus stop, took a ticket for a place 300 kilometres away, a place he had never been to before, nor had he ever wanted to go. Sometimes, when he didn’t know what to do, he chose to travel, because travel, in a way, simulates life, but much faster. You start at a place, with a destination in mind, but you never know who you will meet along the way. Sometimes, the people you meet change the destination you had in mind. But what happens when these people get off at their stops? You are left with a destination that was never yours to travel alone, and a destination which was yours, but which you no longer have any desire to follow. You are left at the mercy of the company of someone who was never in your plans.

John cleared his head and leaned over the window. He preferred a window seat, so that he could watch the scenery pass by, different things marking different cities, every city with its own specific smell, a character that wasn’t present before, that you couldn’t have imagined. But is the new smell always better? He didn’t think so. Nonetheless, he continued staring outside, his mind still in turmoil.

He decided to think all the thoughts his mind was throwing at him, so as to exhaust them, so as to be free from them. Freedom comes to those who persevere, those who try until their death to let go of their demons, realizing that they spent all their lives fighting when they could have surrendered. They would get a shittier life, but at least a life.

So he went ahead and started thinking about the day they met.

He met her one day when she was in his friend’s room. His friend introduced her to John, and at first sight, he couldn’t decide whether he wanted to talk to her, whether he wanted to let himself into the vicinity of someone so awesome. But she was a friend of a friend, and that sure gave her some credibility. He decided to have a conversation.

He sat down on a chair beside her and looked at her, absolutely stunned. As they started conversing, John realized this was not some conversation he had had throughout his life. This was new, exciting, dangerous, everything that went against his urge to survive.

They talked for a long time, of course in a group for now, but John had a hunch that they would continue even later. That day, John felt something he never thought he could—peace.

But this peace was replaced by an ache soon after she left. He had his friends. He had everything he possessed before meeting her. But now, he could feel a fraction of himself left empty without her.

In the following days, they started meeting every day, more than once every day. The only reason John wasn’t with her was because he had to be elsewhere. And it wasn’t just John; everyone around him started to notice the difference too. He had been happier than he had ever been.

In the following month, John understood that he never wanted her out of his life. They would spend the entire day together, sometimes with other friends, sometimes without them. She was the first person he talked to every morning, and the last person he talked to before sleeping. She wasn’t a person anymore; she was a part of his life.

The bus halted, and the conductor came out to announce that they would take a rest stop for half an hour and that people were supposed to get their affairs in order during that time.

John, too, stepped out of the bus, looking everywhere to examine what was around him, but everywhere he looked, he saw her.

But why did he break up with her?

He needed to remind himself of that fact.

Throughout the months that they were together, she was all he needed to face this desolate world. Every time he felt alone, she was there. Every time something scared him, she was there. He had reached a place where the only thought he had was that even if all was lost, he would have her, and they would be happy.

She was a force of nature. Nothing could control her. John had always been reserved about the people he chose to be with, and she was everything beyond that. She made him question his foundations.

The problem with letting your fellow passengers into your life is that you never know what destination you’ll choose in their company. And the destination she had in mind was everything John stood wary of.

If we get one life, should we not spend it doing the things we love? Or should we rather focus on what we should be doing, pertaining to the needs of the people dependent on us?

That was their quarrel.

But John had come to a point where he couldn’t let her go and had to conform to her beliefs, changing his own forever. It had been months now, and he still couldn’t get over her philosophy, even though it made his life worse without her.

Believing is tricky. If the thing you believe in leaves, the belief still doesn’t, and then you are carrying a weight which was never meant to be carried alone. But he owed her that, for some of the best years of his life.

In the following months, he wanted to tell her to think otherwise, but she was too strong in her commitments, more than him, at least.

She was a force of nature.

He never knew if she loved him as much as he did, but he chose to believe it anyway.

One day, John was just strolling in the park, trying to convince himself of the fact that he needed to let her go. Because he couldn’t afford her view of the world.

But then she called, and he folded.

This happened many times throughout the two years they had been together. But still, she never forsook him.

John decided to let himself have her company until he had to move places, and surely enough, in the next year, he had to.

He sat down with her and told her, “I want to start a new life, and I need to focus on my destination.”

She felt sad, but she knew where he came from, so she let him go, on the condition that he never called her.

It wasn’t easy for him, and he knew it.

But that wasn’t the end of the story. He did call her a few times, and every time he called her, everything went back to square one. They would start talking every day and realize they were not perfect for each other.

A month ago, John had his “final breakup.” But today, when he completed his project, he wanted to call her. He was so excited. He knew that if he called her, she would be excited too, but he couldn’t.

He got back on the bus and tried to fall asleep.

He tried to move on from her, but once you taste perfection in life, there aren’t many things that can replace it.

He fell asleep, dreaming of their time together: how she made every boring party a charm with her presence, every boring movie into a masterpiece, how her life spread all around her.

John was woken up by the conductor since he had reached his destination.

Even where he was, he still couldn’t stop thinking about her.

John decided to talk to her once again, for old time’s sake.

He went to the nearest store and asked, “Can I have a Marlboro?”

The shopkeeper gave him the best one, even though all of them were the worst.

He put it to his lips, lit a lighter beneath him, and took a deep drag.

He, once again, got together with his favourite mistake.
